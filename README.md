# 8-2-Journal-Portfolio-Submission

**Briefly summarize The Gaming Room client and their software requirements. Who was the client? What type of software did they want you to design?**

The Gaming Room is a client that focuses on browser and mobile game development, particularly games that seem to be party or multiplayer games. 

They were looking to develop a game, Draw It or Lose It in which teams and players compete against one another to guess drawing subjects before the timer runs out. 

Their design constraints consisted of team or multiplayer support, as well as unique names and IDs for games and players. Only one instance of an ongoing game can exist at any given time, and each player should have a unique identifier for their account. 

**What did you do particularly well in developing this documentation?**

I believe I did particularly well in the development requirements section, where I detailed all of the advantages and disadvantages of many different Operating systems and their different aspects. 

**What about the process of working through a design document did you find helpful when developing the code**?

I found the UML diagram present in the design document particularly helpful when developing the code since it gave me a visual to follow as I was working. If I found myself getting lost in development I could reroute myself by taking a closer look at the UML diagram and my analysis.

**If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?**

I would probably revise my recommendations section. I feel I could've been more precise in my language and discussed the pros and cons of different recommendations more. 

**How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your software design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?**

I interpreted the user's needs based on my knowledge of online gaming. I have experience with a variety of online games from chat games to puzzle games to party games and had an understanding of what sort of features and needs are necessary to improve the gaming experience. I put a special focus on security especially since Draw It or Lose It works on an account-based system and protecting user identities is important. I also made recommendations for hardware and software that were more customizable since creating a unique gaming experience will boost ratings and traffic and thus, funding. Considering the user's needs is so important because in the end the software you are developing is for them, and if their needs are not being met then they may take their business elsewhere. 

**How did you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a similar software application?**

I approached designing the software similar to other online games of the same premise. Skribbl.io in particular was on my mind as I was designing and I took inspiration from its account system and interface when I was designing. I also considered what I enjoy from games, customization in particular, and considered how I could make this similar experience fresh and new to draw in an interested audience. I would use this same referencing strategy in the future when making software because there are no real "original" ideas anymore but instead, putting twists on existing concepts to make them feel different and new.
